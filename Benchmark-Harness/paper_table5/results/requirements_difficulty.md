# Why every phone needs its own profiling run: requirement difficulty without measurements

Generated by `results/graphs/make_requirements.py` from `results/all_results.md` (OnePlus 15, OnePlus 8 Pro, Redmi 9A) and the Galaxy S23 `RESULTS.md` (section 3). Per-configuration verdicts are in `results/requirements_data.json`.

## What we are trying to do

A phone app (or an agent) is asked to run a small language model under a **user-specified requirement**: how fast it must answer, how quickly it must start, how much storage or energy it may use, how capable the model must be. Something must then pick one configuration out of **104 choices per phone: 9 models x 3 precisions (4/8/16-bit) x 2 engines (llama.cpp, MNN) x 2 backends (CPU, GPU)**.

Without measurements the picker can only guess from rules of thumb (smaller is faster, 4-bit is smaller, a GPU is faster). Our four phones show that those rules fail in phone-specific ways:

- The same configuration can pass on one phone and fail on another: an 8 GB phone cannot load 8-bit 4B models that a 14.8 GiB phone runs.
- GPU is not reliably faster: llama.cpp on the OnePlus 8 Pro's Adreno 650 prefills at 0.5-115 tokens/s, slower than its own CPU in all 16 measured cells; MNN GPU decode is slow on the Galaxy S23 and on the 8 Pro, but fast on the OnePlus 15; MNN GPU 16-bit crashes on every phone that has a GPU backend; the Redmi 9A has no OpenCL at all.
- 'Smaller or lower-bit is faster' breaks: llama.cpp Q4_0 files keep a large Q6_K output matrix and the unoptimized OpenCL Q6_K kernel makes some Q4_0 models decode slower than Q8_0 on the GPU; on the Galaxy S23 llama.cpp GPU prefill is 13-53x slower for Q8_0 than for Q4_0 (on the OnePlus 15 the same gap is only 2-4x).
- A profile does not transfer: the set of configurations that meet a requirement on one phone is only partly the set that meets it on another (last column below).

So we run the whole grid **once per phone** and keep the result as a lookup table. The model (or app) then answers 'which configuration meets this requirement on this phone?' from data instead of guessing. The tables below measure how much that profiling run is worth.

## How difficulty is defined

**Blind success rate** = the share of the 104 candidate configurations that satisfy the requirement on a given phone, i.e. the chance that picking one at random without any measurement works. A configuration that crashes, does not fit in memory, or cannot run on that phone counts as a failure (for the user it is one). The levels follow your cut-offs:

| Level | Blind success rate | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | 60-100% | most configurations pass; a priori factors (model size, precision) are nearly enough |
| Medium | 30-60% | about a coin-flip; you must know the phone class and the engine quirks |
| Hard | 0-30% | few configurations pass; without this phone's measurements you almost never guess one |

The level of a requirement in the summary is the average of its four per-phone rates (energy requirements average the three phones that have energy). 'Profile transfer' is the average chance that a configuration which passes on one phone also passes on another phone (averaged over all ordered phone pairs): low numbers mean one phone's profile cannot be reused on a different phone.

Candidate set and thresholds: the 104 configurations every GPU phone has (Qwen3.5-4B F16 was never prepared, so it is excluded everywhere). Decode and prefill thresholds are for a 256-token prompt and 256-token decode. 'Capable' means at least 1.5B parameters (Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3.5-2B, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3.5-4B), used as a quality proxy because accuracy was not measured. Energy is whole-phone, net of idle (the OnePlus 8 Pro has no energy data, so energy requirements are not scored on it). Storage is the weight-file size.

## What the numbers say

- **Easy (3):** R1 (61%); R13 (66%); R14 (71%). A blind pick usually works, but only because the thresholds are low.
- **Medium (7):** R2 (37%); R3 (51%); R4 (47%); R5 (33%); R6 (39%); R7 (34%); R15 (54%). Whether the pick works depends on the phone.
- **Hard (5):** R8 (14%); R9 (16%); R10 (7%); R11 (7%); R12 (6%). Combined requirements (capable model AND fast AND frugal) leave only a handful of passing configurations per phone, and they are not the same handful on each phone.

- The cheap rules of thumb are not enough on their own: the passing set on every phone mixes engines, backends and precisions, so no single 'always use X' policy passes everywhere.

## Caveats

- The Redmi 9A has no OpenCL, so its 52 GPU configurations count as failures; that makes it look harder than a CPU-only comparison would. Counting only its 52 CPU configurations the blind success rates are: R1 23%, R2 0%, R3 52%, R4 4%, R5 0%, R6 19%, R7 0%, R8 0%, R9 0%, R10 0%, R11 0%, R13 44%, R14 71%, R15 12%, R12 0%.
- Accuracy (answer quality) was never measured, so 'capable' is a parameter-count proxy, not a quality result.
- OnePlus 15 / 8 Pro / Redmi energy and the Galaxy S23 energy come from different instruments (Perfetto battery gauge for OnePlus 15 and S23, sysfs battery current for the Redmi), all whole-phone net of idle. The 8 Pro has none.
- A small number of OnePlus 15 cells are suspected slow-governor runs (for example Qwen3-0.6B llama.cpp 8-bit CPU and Qwen3.5-4B llama.cpp Q4_0 CPU), and the S23 file lists its own cells to treat with caution; I used every value as reported.
- Thresholds are my realistic choices; change them in `REQS` in `make_requirements.py` and rerun.
